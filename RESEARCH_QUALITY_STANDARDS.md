# Research Quality Standards

This file defines a simple quality system for reviewing sources and research notes.

The goal is to keep the repository disciplined, evidence-based, and honest about uncertainty.

## Rating scale

### 1 = Weak / Unverified
Use this when:
- the source is unclear or anonymous
- the claim is unsupported
- the source is difficult to verify
- the information is incomplete or obviously weak
- there is no clear attribution or context

Examples:
- rumor-heavy content
- unsupported social posts
- vague claims without evidence
- links without clear source or purpose

### 2 = Low Quality / Limited Value
Use this when:
- the source has some relevance but weak documentation
- the information is partial or dated
- the claim may be useful but is not strongly proven
- the source is not easily corroborated

Examples:
- weak blog content
- outdated public posts
- low-trust content with limited verification

### 3 = Useful with Caution
Use this when:
- the source is relevant and moderately credible
- the information is somewhat supported
- the thesis is reasonable but not fully corroborated
- the source is useful as a clue, not as proof

Examples:
- a useful article with limited sourcing
- a public source that aligns with other information
- a social profile that helps contextualize a claim but still needs confirmation

### 4 = Strong and Credible
Use this when:
- the source is clearly attributable
- the information is well-supported
- the claim is consistent with other trusted sources
- the source is relevant and useful for research

Examples:
- official company pages
- public records or institutional publications
- reputable journalism with clear sourcing
- strong public-source analysis with verification

### 5 = Highly Reliable and Corroborated
Use this when:
- the source is strong, clear, and well-documented
- multiple independent sources align
- the claim is supported by direct evidence or strong corroboration
- the source is both relevant and highly trustworthy

Examples:
- official government or institutional sources
- highly reliable reporting with strong evidence
- multiple corroborating public records or documents

---

## How to use the scale
When reviewing a source, ask:
- Is it public and accessible?
- Is it relevant to the research question?
- Is the author or organization identifiable?
- Does it provide evidence or supporting context?
- Can it be checked elsewhere?
- Does it match other sources?
- Is there uncertainty or contradiction?

Assign a score based on the strongest honest answer.

---

## Decision rules
- 4 or 5 = strong enough to use as a real reference
- 3 = useful but needs more cross-checking
- 1 or 2 = weak, uncertain, or likely not useful without stronger support

---

## Documentation rule
Every source should ideally include:
- source name
- source type
- date reviewed
- relevance
- reliability score
- confidence level
- notes on verification or missing context

---

## Research quality principle
The goal is not to pretend certainty where none exists.

Good research says:
- what is confirmed
- what is likely
- what is uncertain
- what still needs checking

This is how you reduce hallucinations, weak sourcing, and overconfidence.

---

## Final rule
If a source is weak, unclear, or unsupported, do not treat it as fact.
Use it only as a clue and note its limitations clearly.
