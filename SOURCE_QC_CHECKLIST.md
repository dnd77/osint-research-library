# Source QC Checklist

Use this checklist before adding a source, article, tool, or claim to the repository.

## 1. Source exists and is public
- Does the URL resolve?
- Is the source public and accessible?
- Is the organization or author clearly identified?
- Does the source appear to be legitimate and stable? 

## 2. Source is relevant
- Does it actually relate to OSINT, public-source research, investigation, or methodology?
- Is it directly useful for the topic or category being researched?
- Is it more than just a random or unrelated link?

## 3. Source quality is reasonable
- Is it from an official, reputable, or well-known organization?
- Is it a publication, government source, institutional site, well-known newsroom, or a strong research project?
- Does the content look professionally produced and appropriately sourced?

## 4. Information is supported
- Are claims backed by evidence, references, or documentation?
- Does the content cite primary or secondary sources?
- Are dates and context clearly shown?

## 5. Bias and limitations are considered
- Does the source describe its limitations or uncertainty?
- Is the source neutral, transparent, and careful?
- Does it avoid unsupported speculation or sensational language?

## 6. Context is clear
- Is the date clear?
- Is the author or organization identified?
- Is the topic and scope clearly defined?
- Does the source provide enough detail to understand the claim?

## 7. Verification is possible
- Can the claim be checked elsewhere?
- Is there an independent corroborating source?
- Does the claim match public records, official reporting, or a trusted source?

## 8. Ethics and safety
- Is the source aligned with public, ethical, and legal OSINT practices?
- Does it avoid collecting private or restricted information?
- Does it stay within safe and legitimate public-source research norms?

## 9. Keep notes on confidence
For every source, record:
- source name
- source type
- date reviewed
- relevance score
- reliability score
- confidence level
- whether it was corroborated

## 10. Reject or flag weak items
Flag or reject a source if:
- it is not clearly attributable
- it is anonymous and unverifiable
- it contains unsupported or sensational claims
- it is outdated without context
- it is unrelated or obviously weak

## Recommended review standard
Use this simple rule:
- Strong source = official, dated, clear, corroborated, relevant
- Weak source = anonymous, vague, conflicting, unsupported, or irrelevant

## Final QC rule
Before adding a source to the repository, ask:
- Does this source actually exist?
- Is it public and relevant?
- Is it trustworthy enough to be useful?
- Can this be verified by another source if needed?

If the answer is not clearly yes, do not add it without more review.
